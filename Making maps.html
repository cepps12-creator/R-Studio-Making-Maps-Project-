c.) Modify your thematic map in 2b, changing from a sequential color scheme to a divergent color scheme. How does the new choice affect the map? Does the original or modified approach better represent the data? Why?

Europe_Birth_Map <- Europe_Birth_Map |>
  mutate(Birth_Rate_Category = cut(
    Crude_birth_rate,
    breaks = c(0, 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, Inf),
    labels = c("Extremely Low", "Very Low", "Low", "Moderate", "High", "Very High"),
    ordered_result = TRUE
  ))

ggplot(data = Europe_Birth_Map) +
  geom_sf(aes(fill = Birth_Rate_Category), color = "black") +
  scale_fill_brewer(palette = "RdYlBu") + 
  theme_minimal() +
  labs(title = "Europe Countries by Crude Birth Rate (Divergent Scheme)",
       fill = "Birth Rate Category")

  • This choice effects my map by showing that the range of crude births in Europe is low in general compared to global birth rate category ranges. The second map better represents my data because it gives more insight and dimension into how the data shows that Europe is a low crude birth continent rather than just showing the ranges between countries. The first map fails to highlight key extremes of how low the birth rates are.
